XREAL vs Rokid vs RayNeo: Understanding the Display Glasses Race

XREAL, Rokid and TCL RayNeo are important names in the consumer display-glasses conversation. Their products and capabilities vary, so it is worth comparing what each device actually displays, how it connects and whether it augments the world or creates a private virtual screen.

By ARGlasses Editorial Desk · Published 1 October 2026 · Updated 1 October 2026 · 6 min read
AR GLASSESMANUFACTURER COMPARISON

First, identify the product category

Some glasses create a large private virtual screen for films, games or desktop work. Others add sensors, cameras or spatial tracking. A smaller subset uses optical see-through displays to place graphics over the real world. Marketing terms can overlap, so check the technical description of each specific model rather than relying on the word “AR” alone.

XREAL: wearable screens and spatial experiences

XREAL has built a profile around glasses that connect to phones, computers and gaming devices to provide a personal display. When comparing a model, check supported devices, connection requirements, electrochromic or dimming features, tracking support and whether spatial features require an accessory or compatible host.

Rokid: AI and display-led eyewear

Rokid spans wearable display products and AI-oriented glasses. The distinction between its product families matters: camera-and-audio assistance, a monocular display and binocular spatial display products deliver different experiences. Buyers should check whether a product includes a display, what kind it uses and which functions run independently.

TCL RayNeo: consumer display experimentation

RayNeo is part of TCL's wider display and consumer-electronics activity. Its range has included products with different display approaches and feature sets. Check each model's optical design, brightness, field of view, refresh rate, compatibility and software support before comparing it with another brand.

How to compare them fairly

Compare like with like: display type, resolution per eye, field of view, brightness, refresh rate, weight, nose-pad comfort, prescription support, audio, tracking, connection method, battery arrangement, operating-system support, warranty and price. A high-resolution virtual monitor is not automatically a transparent AR experience.

Our outlook

The category is likely to keep splitting into specialist products: lightweight private displays, AI-enabled everyday eyewear and more ambitious see-through AR. Manufacturers that explain those differences clearly — and make devices comfortable and easy to use — will make it easier for customers to choose.
