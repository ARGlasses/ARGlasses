Each company is approaching everyday eyewear from a different angle. These are the announced directions; the predictions below are analysis, not confirmed launch promises.

What happens next?

The confirmed moves — and the signals they may send about the future of AR eyewear.

01 · Meta · Confirmed updates + outlook

Meta is moving from hands-free AI towards information in your line of sight

At Connect on 23 September, Meta announced new features for Meta Ray-Ban Display: hands-free cycling and public-transport directions, a personalised hologram for video calls, an Explore app and plans for mirrored phone notifications. Meta also announced expansion into the UK, Canada, France, Italy and Germany.

Why it could be exciting: navigation is a natural fit for see-through displays. Instead of repeatedly checking a phone, directions can appear when needed while the wearer stays aware of the street. Holographic calling points towards a more expressive kind of communication, although comfort, battery life and social acceptance will determine how often people actually use it.

Our outlook: Meta’s roadmap suggests a gradual bridge from camera-and-audio glasses to glasses that can place useful visual information directly in front of you. The important test is whether those features feel effortless rather than distracting.

Primary source: Meta — New Features for Meta Ray-Ban Display, 23 September 2026.

02 · Google · Confirmed roadmap + outlook

Google wants Gemini to understand what you see — and eventually show you what you need

Google’s Android XR announcement at I/O 2026 described two kinds of intelligent eyewear. Audio glasses, developed with eyewear partners including Gentle Monster and Warby Parker, are designed for spoken Gemini assistance, navigation, translation, messages and camera-based questions about the world. Google said audio glasses would arrive first, with display glasses following later.

Why it could be exciting: Google has the ingredients to connect glasses to maps, search, translation and Android services. If the display version can present just the right detail at the right moment, glasses could become a genuinely useful companion for travel, learning and getting things done — without turning every moment into a screen session.

Our outlook: Watch how quickly Google moves from voice-first products to a comfortable, lightweight display. Its Android XR strategy could matter beyond one device if it attracts developers and eyewear brands.

Primary source: Google — Intelligent eyewear is coming this fall, 19 May 2026.

03 · Snap · Confirmed roadmap + outlook

Snap is betting that the killer feature will be a world full of useful AR experiences

Snap’s 16 September update for Specs introduced new experiences across entertainment, education, work, shopping, translation, navigation and fitness, alongside Specs Intelligence. The company also detailed partnerships with enterprise technology providers including Salesforce, Nvidia and AWS. Specs is designed as a standalone see-through AR computer, with voice and hand interaction.

Why it could be exciting: Snap’s approach points to one of AR’s most compelling possibilities: digital instructions, creative tools and shared experiences that appear anchored to the real world. Imagine learning a practical skill with visual prompts in place, or collaborating around a 3D object that everyone can see from their own angle.

Our outlook: The big question is whether Snap can attract enough great software and make the hardware light and affordable enough for regular use. If it can, Specs could help move AR from occasional novelty to a new way of interacting with information and people.

Primary source: Snap — SPECS Make Computing More Human, 16 September 2026. Additional reporting: Reuters, 16 September 2026.
